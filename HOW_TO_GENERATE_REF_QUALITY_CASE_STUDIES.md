# Guide: How to Generate REF-Quality Impact Case Studies with Aston AI Research Tool

## What is REF Impact?

REF (Research Excellence Framework) defines impact as:

> "An effect, change or benefit to the economy, society, culture, public policy or services, health, the environment or quality of life **beyond academia**."

The key is: **Your research must have REAL-WORLD consequences for people/organizations outside academia.**

## The Three Pillars of REF Impact

### 1. **REACH** - Who Benefited?
You must answer clearly:
- How many beneficiaries? (specific number or range)
- What type? (patients, companies, government, communities)
- Where? (local, national, international)

❌ **Poor**: "The tool was adopted by several organizations"  
✅ **Good**: "500+ NHS patients benefited across 12 hospital departments in the UK"

### 2. **SIGNIFICANCE** - How Much Did It Matter?
You must show quantifiable change:
- % improvement
- £ cost savings / new revenue
- Time saved (hours/days)
- Policy changes adopted
- Lives improved
- Duration of impact (months/years)

❌ **Poor**: "Improved efficiency"  
✅ **Good**: "Reduced diagnostic time by 40% (3 weeks faster), enabling earlier treatment for 500+ patients annually. Documented £2 million cost savings over 18 months."

### 3. **EVIDENCE** - How Do You Know It Worked?
Your impact must be verifiable:
- Published in peer-reviewed journal ✓
- Case study from reputable source ✓
- Policy document or government report ✓
- Independent media coverage ✓
- University/hospital verification ✓
- ❌ Vendor marketing claims alone ❌
- ❌ Unverifiable internal reports ❌

## Five Steps to Generate REF-Quality Results

### Step 1: Start with a Strong Prompt

**Formula for Good Prompts:**
```
[Research topic] + [Real-world application] + [Quantified outcome] + [Beneficiary type]
```

**Examples:**

❌ **Weak Prompts**:
- "AI in healthcare"
- "Renewable energy research"
- "Blockchain applications"

✅ **Strong Prompts**:
- "AI diagnostic tools adopted by healthcare providers with documented patient outcome improvements (>5% accuracy gain, >100 patients)"
- "Renewable energy cost reduction studies with documented adoption by companies/utilities (>15% cost reduction, >50 organizations)"
- "Blockchain supply chain implementations with documented benefit metrics (time saved, cost reduction, adoption by enterprises)"

### Step 2: Include Specific Metrics in Your Prompt

Add quantitative targets:
```
"Show me cases where [technology] led to:
- [X]% improvement in [metric]
- £[Y] cost savings
- [Z]+ organizations/people adopting it
- Evidence: [type of documentation needed]
- Recent: implemented 2023-2024"
```

**Example:**
```
"AI tools that improved drug discovery timelines by >20%, with evidence from 
pharmaceutical companies or research institutions, adoption by >10 pharma firms, 
published case studies or white papers, implemented 2023-2024"
```

### Step 3: Specify Beneficiary Type

Be clear about WHO benefits:

✅ **Good**:
- "Patient impact: faster diagnosis, improved outcomes"
- "Healthcare worker impact: reduced administrative burden"
- "Hospital impact: cost savings, efficiency gains"
- "Policy impact: government regulations adopted"

❌ **Bad**:
- "General impact" (too vague)
- "Internal use only" (not REF-eligible)
- "Theoretical application" (not verified)

### Step 4: Ask for Reach & Significance

Include these magic words in prompts:
- "**Reach**: How many beneficiaries? Geographic scope?"
- "**Significance**: Quantified outcome metrics? Duration of impact?"
- "**Evidence**: Verifiable sources? Independent corroboration?"

**Better Prompt Example:**
```
"Find AI implementations in healthcare with:
- REACH: 100+ patients affected, multi-site deployment (>3 hospitals)
- SIGNIFICANCE: 30%+ improvement in clinical metrics, 12+ months sustained
- EVIDENCE: Published case studies, peer-reviewed journals, or official hospital reports
- Recent: 2022-2024 implementations
- Geographic: UK or European deployment"
```

### Step 5: Review Results Against REF Checklist

For each case study the tool generates, verify:

| Criterion | REF Requirement | Your Case Study |
|-----------|-----------------|-----------------|
| **Beneficiary Type** | Named external organization/community? | ☐ Yes ☐ No |
| **Beneficiary Count** | Specific number or range (not "several")? | ☐ Yes ☐ No |
| **Geographic Scope** | Location clear (local/national/international)? | ☐ Yes ☐ No |
| **Quantified Outcome** | At least one metric (%, £, time, adoption rate)? | ☐ Yes ☐ No |
| **Outcome Scale** | Is it meaningful (>5% improvement or £100K+)? | ☐ Yes ☐ No |
| **Duration** | Evidence of sustained impact (>6 months)? | ☐ Yes ☐ No |
| **Beyond-Academia** | Real-world external impact (not just publication)? | ☐ Yes ☐ No |
| **Verifiable Source** | Published, documented, or independently verified? | ☐ Yes ☐ No |

✅ **All checks YES?** = REF-Quality case study!

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

### ❌ Mistake 1: Too Vague
**Bad**: "AI is being used in finance"  
**Fix**: Add specifics: "JPMorgan uses AI to process 10,000 loan applications daily, reducing review time by 60%, reaching 100,000+ applicants annually"

### ❌ Mistake 2: No Numbers
**Bad**: "Tool improves productivity"  
**Fix**: Add metrics: "Tool reduced processing time from 2 hours to 30 minutes (75% improvement), deployed across 5 departments, affecting 200+ employees daily"

### ❌ Mistake 3: Only Internal Impact
**Bad**: "Our team used it internally"  
**Fix**: Specify external benefit: "Our team used it to serve 10,000 external customers, reducing their costs by £5 million annually"

### ❌ Mistake 4: Only Academic
**Bad**: "We published a paper on this technology"  
**Fix**: Show real-world use: "Our research was adopted by 50 organizations, leading to 30% efficiency improvement across the sector"

### ❌ Mistake 5: Unverifiable Claims
**Bad**: "Tool reportedly improved outcomes"  
**Fix**: Add evidence: "Tool improvement documented in peer-reviewed publication (Journal X, 2024) and verified case study from Hospital Y"

## Questions & Answers

**Q: Does my research need to be published to count as impact?**  
A: No - but impact must be documented. Published papers, case studies, policy documents, media coverage, and independent testimonials all count. Purely internal or unverifiable claims do not.

**Q: How recent should case studies be?**  
A: REF typically looks at last 6-7 years. For this tool: prefer 2-3 year window to ensure impact is recent and sustained.

**Q: Do student impacts count?**  
A: YES! REF includes teaching and professional practice impacts. A case study about improved student learning through your research is valid REF impact.

**Q: Does pure academic knowledge count?**  
A: NO - Advancing academic knowledge alone is not REF impact. There must be a benefit beyond academia (economic, social, policy, health, environmental, cultural).

**Q: What if my impact is smaller scale?**  
A: Even small-scale impacts count if they're well-documented and verifiable. 20 small companies adopting your tool is valid. Impact quality matters as much as scale.
